Brewing guide

Brew better at home.

Three timeless methods, each with a clear ratio and simple steps — so every cup is a good one.

South‑Indian Filter

The traditional kaapi method — rich, bold, and decadent.

Ratio 1:420 g coffee · 80 ml water
  1. Grind coffee fine — slightly coarser than espresso, finer than drip.
  2. Fill the upper chamber with 20 g of ground coffee and gently tamp with the press disc.
  3. Pour 80 ml of just-off-boil water (96 °C) over the grounds.
  4. Let the decoction drip through — 8–12 minutes. Do not rush it.
  5. Warm 80 ml milk, add a teaspoon of jaggery or sugar, and pour the decoction over. Froth by pulling between two cups.

French Press

Full-bodied immersion — honest and forgiving.

Ratio 1:1530 g coffee · 450 ml water
  1. Grind coffee coarse — sea-salt texture. Pre-warm the press with hot water.
  2. Add 30 g of ground coffee into the carafe.
  3. Start a timer and pour 450 ml of just-off-boil water (94–96 °C) evenly over the grounds.
  4. At 1:00, stir the crust gently with a wooden spoon and place the lid on.
  5. At 4:00, plunge slowly — about 20 seconds — and serve immediately. Do not let it sit or it will over-extract.

Pour-Over

Clean, bright, and precise — for the ritualist.

Ratio 1:1620 g coffee · 320 ml water
  1. Grind coffee medium-fine — like table salt. Rinse the paper filter and preheat the dripper.
  2. Add 20 g of ground coffee to the filter and level the bed with a gentle shake.
  3. Bloom: start your timer and pour 40 ml of water (96 °C). Let it degas for 30–45 seconds.
  4. Pour in slow, concentric spirals — keep the stream steady and avoid the paper edges. Total pour: 320 ml.
  5. Drawdown should finish around 2:45–3:15. If it takes longer, coarsen your grind next time. Swirl and serve.

All ratios start from whole-bean weight. Use a scale, not a scoop — consistency is the difference between a good cup and a great one.
